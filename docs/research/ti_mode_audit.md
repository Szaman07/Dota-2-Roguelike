# Audit: a full TI run instead of the boss gauntlet (research only)

_**Decided 21:43 UTC:** switch to the TI run; picks in the group stage only (loss 2, win 1); era factor removed; 5-slot bench; playoff wins give variant upgrades. The current design is in [game_design.md](game_design.md) revision 5._

_Date: 2026-10-11, 21:50 UTC. Responds to Sadman's 21:32 idea. Builds on [game_design.md](game_design.md) (revision 4), whose draft, ratings, bench and dice sim all still apply. Tags: VERIFIED = checked live, PROTOTYPE = throwaway Python in my scratch space, INFERRED, OPINION._

## 1. The idea
Instead of fighting fixed bosses in order, you play an actual TI:
- **Group stage:** each win gives you 2 picks, each loss 1 pick.
- **Your placing** puts you in the upper bracket, the lower bracket, or out.
- **Bracket:** double elimination to the grand final.
- **The field** comes from a pool of memorable rosters: **winners and finalists of tier-1 tournaments only**.

## 2. Honest take (OPINION)
**I think it's better than the gauntlet, and you're right about the bosses.** In the gauntlet, a boss was just a strong roster with a real draft and no special rule. That's exactly what a strong TI team is, so a TI loses nothing and gains a lot:
- **Real Dota drama:** the group table, the upper/lower bracket split, a lower-bracket run. Double elimination forgives one bad series, which suits a dice game better than "lose once and the run is over".
- **Variety:** every run has a different field instead of the same 7 bosses.
- **Less hand-made content:** no boss list, act structure or era-by-era underlings to maintain. The pool is generated by a query.
- **Better database material** (§6).

What you lose: the scripted climb from 2011 to 2026 and a guaranteed final showdown with a legendary team. Both can be partly kept (§5).

## 3. The pool: winners and finalists of tier-1 events
VERIFIED partly (OpenDota, 21:45 UTC): the final match of each OpenDota "premium" event, after removing qualifiers and Division II and lower leagues, gives **111 events → 222 finalist lineups**. Lineups sharing 4+ players within 2 years count as the same roster, which leaves **133 distinct rosters, 73 of them with a title**. That list is skewed: it's heavy on 2021–23 regional DPC leagues and thin before 2016 and after 2024, because of how OpenDota labels events. Liquipedia's tier-1 list (decided as the pool source) fixes the coverage. I'd expect **a few hundred** distinct rosters (INFERRED), plenty for a 16-team field that's different every run.

Each pool roster also brings **its real draft from the final it played**, so "teams play their real drafts" carries over from the boss design.

## 4. The run, and what the numbers say (PROTOTYPE)
Format I tested (my default; real TIs changed format almost every year):
- **16 teams, 2 groups of 8, Bo1 round robin** (7 games each).
- Places 1–4 go to the upper bracket, 5–6 to the lower bracket, 7–8 are out.
- Upper bracket Bo3; lower bracket starts Bo1 then Bo3; grand final Bo5.

20,000 runs per line, using the per-game win chances from the dice sim (§4 of game_design.md). "Growth" means each pick improves your team a little (+0.5% win chance per pick, a rough guess):

| Your per-game win chance | Champion (no growth) | Champion (with growth) | Out in groups | Games in a run (median) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 45% (weaker than the field) | 6% | 15% | ~30% | 10–11 |
| 50% (even) | 12% | 26% | ~22% | 12–14 |
| 55% | 21% | 40% | ~16% | 14–15 |
| 62% (+5 rating) | 39% | 62% | ~9% | 15–16 |

For comparison, the gauntlet's first slice (3 Bo1 + a Bo3 boss) was won **6% / 10% / 16%** of the time at 50% / 55% / 62%.

**Issue found: picks.** If every game in the whole run gives picks, a run hands out a median **17–27 picks** on top of the 10-pick draft. That's a lot of screens, and it snowballs the team. Your message ties picks to the **group stage**, which gives **7–14 picks**, a sensible number. The bracket would then give none and be pure execution, with bench swaps between series. That's my default reading.

**Also worth deciding:** a loss still gives a pick, so a bad group stage isn't hopeless. But the gap between winning (2) and losing (1) is small. If wins should matter more, 2 vs 0 or 3 vs 1 are alternatives.

## 5. What carries over, and how to keep the "boss" feeling
| Piece | In a TI run |
|---|---|
| Opening draft, bench, ratings, era cards, dice sim, counters, drafts shown before games | Unchanged |
| Era factor | **Needs a decision.** With it on, 2011–2014 rosters become easy group-stage wins in a mixed-era field. With it off, every pool roster plays at its own peak, an "all-time TI" where any matchup is fair |
| Bosses | Become **seeds**: e.g. put the strongest rosters (TI winners) as top seeds in the other group, so you likely meet one in the bracket |
| OG / Spirit finale | Optional: a fixed grand-final opponent (e.g. Spirit TI15) if you want a guaranteed showdown |
| Underlings | Gone; the rest of the field takes their place |
| First slice | One group of 8 plus a short bracket (top 4 → semis → final) |

## 6. Database implications
Better course material than the gauntlet:
- **Self-referencing foreign key:** each bracket match points to the match its winner (and its loser) goes to next (`next_win_match_id`, `next_lose_match_id`). That's a classic schema lesson and easy to draw in the ER diagram.
- **Standings as a VIEW with window functions:** `RANK() OVER (PARTITION BY group ORDER BY wins DESC, ...)` plus tiebreakers.
- **Transactions:** recording a series result and advancing both teams in the bracket must happen together. Seeds are stored first so a refresh can't re-roll a loss.
- **The whole field is simulated:** the other 15 teams' games are simulated too (about 56 group games plus the bracket). That's cheap but means a few hundred `run_game` rows per run, which is good data for queries such as "which pool rosters win most often".

New entities: `tournament_run`, `run_team` (the 16 entrants and seeds), `run_group_game`, `bracket_match` (with the two self-references), `pool_roster` (winners and finalists, each with its final draft).

## 7. Risks
- **Bo1 groups are swingy:** at even strength you're out in groups about 1 run in 4. Bo2 groups (old TI style) are fairer but double the games.
- **Run length:** 10–16 of your own games per run, versus 4 in the gauntlet slice. The sim is instant, so the real cost is replay watching, which can be skipped.
- **Tiebreakers** in groups need a rule (head-to-head, then a tiebreaker game or a coin flip).
- **Pool cleanup:** the same core reaches many finals, so rosters must be grouped (I used 4+ shared players within 2 years) to avoid near-duplicate teams in one field.
